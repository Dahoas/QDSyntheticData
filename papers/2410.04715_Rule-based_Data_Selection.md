# [Rule-based Data Selection for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04715)

ID: 36

## Summary

They introduce an automated, rule-based framework for selecting high-quality LLM data, utilizing
LLMs to generate a diverse set of rules and the determinantal point process (DPP) method to eliminate redundancy. 
It is a rule-based framework that utilizes the orthogonality of score vectors associated
with rules as a novel metric for rule evaluations requiring no human intervention in designing and selecting
rules. 

Their automated pipeline first uses LLMs to generate a diverse set of rules, encompassing various rating dimensions to evaluate data
quality. Then it rates a batch of data based on these rules and uses the determinantal point process
(DPP) from random matrix theory to select the most orthogonal score vectors, thereby identifying a
set of independent rules used to evaluate all data to selevt for training.

## Results

- They validate their method through two approaches: A) comparison
with ground truth ratings, and B) training LLMs with selected data and assessing performance across
various benchmarks. The experiments span multiple models, including Pythia-1B and Llama3-8B
(fine-tuned with LoRA), and cover diverse domains such as IMDB, Medical, Math, and Code.

They find that using rule-based rating is mostly guaranteed to give better
results than rating without rules, in their experiments applied to general data (CommonCrawl) and domain-specific
data (IMDB). 

They benchmark their method against several baselines,
including random selection, direct rating without rules, QuRating rules (Wettig et al., 2024), and DSIR (Xie
et al., 2024).

*Tasks:*

* General Continued Pre-training:  Continued pre-training of Pythia-1B for 20k samples. Performs convincingly better than baselines on benchmarks. 
* Domain-specifc Fine-tuning on four domains: IMDB, Medical, Math, and Code. They select 20K domain-related data samples,
* and see significant improvements compared to general continued pre-training. Rule-based methods consistently
outperform rule-free approaches in general, especially when comparing against Random Select and No Rule. DPP outperforms other rule-based methods.

## Other comments

* Experiments demonstrate that fine-grained rule-
based rating outperforms rule-free methods, producing more precise data quality assessments, leading to
improved benchmark performance of LLMs.

* They introduce a novel rule evaluation metric designed to promote low correlation and high diversity among rules. We propose the method of using DPP (determinant point process) on task-aware rule-rating
vectors to select a subset of independent rules.

* The entire process operates independently of human heuristics and is free from human biases. Their automated pipeline generates the rules,
selects the rules, and then chooses data according to rule-based ratings.
